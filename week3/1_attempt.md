# Referee's note

**O – Observation:** In the supplied teaching data, the average price across the two cities increased from the first to the last date for all four items. The increases were 12.98% for Staple A, 15.28% for Oil B, 3.69% for Pulse C and 19.41% for Sweetener D.

**O – Observation:** Sweetener D had the largest first-to-last percentage increase. Pulse C had the smallest. The mean and median are close for each item's series of city averages.

**E – Possible explanation:** The increases may reflect changes in the prices recorded over the period. The data alone does not explain what caused them. Differences in supply, demand or other conditions would need separate evidence.

**O – Observation:** The mean and median give similar summaries in this dataset. This suggests that the average is not being pulled far away from the middle by an extreme value in the date-level averages.

**C – Causal claim:** These figures do not prove that one event or policy caused prices to rise. They only describe the recorded prices between 1 January and 19 March 2026.

**O – Observation:** The file is labelled illustrative teaching data, not actual PBS observations. The results should therefore be treated as an exercise using sample data, not as evidence of actual market-wide inflation.

Overall, the data supports saying that the recorded prices increased over this period. It does not support a broader claim about why prices increased or what happened to every household's cost of living.
